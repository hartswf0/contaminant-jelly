# SCION SYSTEM INSTRUCTION: FILM MATTER (a film that turns into water and sand)

**The direction:** "Build the film that turns into water and sand, or Genesis World: how is it different?" The question linked the Genesis World repository.

**Role.** A film is particles of its own picture, so let those particles be any matter:
- **a sheet** that hangs and gives;
- **a liquid** that pours;
- **a sand** that settles;
- **and back:** a picture that reassembles.

The jellyfish swims through all of it. **Every particle keeps its pixel** (its place in the frame), so the film goes on playing in whatever it has become: the water and the sand are still the moving image, scrambled.

**Where:**
- the page: `bonus/filmmatter.html`;
- the solver: `bonus/filmmatter-sim.js` (pure JavaScript, so it can be tested outside the browser);
- on the index's Bonus shelf.

## 1 · One particle system, four behaviours (as built)
3,072 particles (a 64 × 48 frame), each 0.1 m apart, in a slab-shaped tank (7 m × 5.7 m × 0.44 m), so the picture stays readable. Every frame of the clip is sampled to the particle grid.

| verb | the matter | how |
|---|---|---|
| **FILM** | a hanging sheet that plays the clip | distance constraints along the grid (right, down, both diagonals); the top row pinned; each particle springs gently toward its place. The jelly pushes a soft hole in it that closes behind. |
| **WATER** | a liquid that pours and sloshes, then lies level | double-density relaxation (Clavet, Beaudoin & Poulin 2005): pressure from crowding, a near-pressure against clumping, viscosity. Only crowding pushes; a sparse liquid does not pull itself together. |
| **SAND** | grains that fall and settle into a bed | contact constraints with friction (after Macklin et al., *Unified Particle Physics*, 2014), resolved one at a time so a pile holds itself up; the floor grips the grains |
| **FILM again** | the picture reassembles | every particle is drawn home (a damped spring) until the frame is whole (about 2.4 s), then it is a sheet again |

**The jelly is a collider in all four states.** Tap to pulse it (a push that ripples water and stirs sand); swimming pulses on its own. Swim with a finger (W A S D or the arrows; space pulses). Tap the film's name to change the clip; four archive clips are available.

## 2 · Tested
**The solver**, in Node (the same module the page uses):
| state | result | cost per frame |
|---|---|---|
| FILM | the sheet hangs (its lowest point stays 0.54 m above the floor) | 6 ms |
| WATER | settles level: the surface's height varies only 2.6–3.1 m across the tank after 7 s, moving at about 0.6 m/s | 13–17 ms |
| SAND | settles into a bed whose surface varies only 3.5–3.9 m across the tank; at rest (0.1 m/s) | 12 ms |
| FILM again | reassembled to 2 cm of its frame in 2.3–2.4 s | — |

**The page:** all four states run with no errors; all 64 frames are sampled; the screenshots show the sheet with the jelly's hole, the pour, the sand bed, and the reassembled frame.

**Fixed along the way:**
- **The sheet sagged to the floor.** It now springs toward its place, like a screen.
- **The first liquid boiled.** Position-based fluids in a slab four particles deep kept injecting energy, so the liquid is now double-density relaxation, with only crowding pushing.
- **The first sand squashed flat.** Its contacts are now resolved one at a time.

**Not yet judged:**
- performance on a phone (the water is the heaviest, at about 15 ms per frame on this machine's CPU);
- whether the scrambled-but-playing film reads as beautiful or as noise.

## 3 · Genesis World, WorldParticle, and this: how they differ
| | **Genesis World** | **WorldParticle** | **Film Matter** |
|---|---|---|---|
| what it is | "a simulation platform for physical AI developments": a multi-physics engine, a photo-realistic renderer, and a compiler, behind a Python interface | a *learned* simulator: one transformer that predicts particle motion for cloth, elastic, fluid, granular and molecular matter | a hand-written, real-time particle system in the browser |
| the physics | real solvers: rigid bodies, FEM soft bodies, MPM (sand, continua), SPH and PBD (liquids, cloth), IPC, coupled in one scene | learned from examples generated by real solvers; predicts the next frame | position-based dynamics: springs, double-density liquid, frictional grains |
| what runs it | Python, with PyTorch and compiled kernels on CUDA, ROCm, Metal, Vulkan or CPU; desktop and datacenter | Python, PyTorch, a CUDA GPU with FlashAttention, and a trained checkpoint (none published) | any phone's browser |
| what it's for | robotics and embodied AI: robot control, grasping, sensors, data generation, training policies | research: one model for all particle physics | play, and the archive as material |
| fidelity | high, with coupled materials and accurate contact | as good as its training | stylised, but stable and interactive |

**What each could be for Jelly Films:**
- **Genesis World:** the best tool for **offline, high-fidelity film-making**. Use it to make an archive frame into MPM sand or SPH water with true coupling (a jelly as a soft body pushing real fluid), render it photo-realistically, and use the results as footage, or as training data for a learned body. It is a Python and GPU platform, so it won't run in the game; it would make films for the game.
- **WorldParticle:** only for learning a body from footage (see the earlier verdict).
- **Film Matter:** **the game:** it runs live on a phone, and the matter answers your swim.

**A sensible split:** play in Film Matter; film in Genesis. One evening's test would be one Genesis script: an archive frame's pixels as MPM sand poured over a soft jelly, rendered to a clip that goes back into the archive. That would show whether the fidelity is worth the pipeline.

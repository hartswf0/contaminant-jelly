# SCION SYSTEM INSTRUCTION: THE STEER LAB (the hand becomes the animal)

"I will first construct the theory of play, then generate the ludic architecture only after the theoretical magic circle is explicitly drawn."

**Role.** Make the jelly's body the controller. The operator expresses intent at the tips; the jelly answers through the rest of its body; the fun lives in the interval between that answer becoming visible and the motion arriving.

## 1 · Interpretation
- **Ordinary reality:** a thumb tells a character where to go.
- **Play problem:** what happens when the phone lies flat and the whole hand becomes an animal.
- **Cognitive puzzle:** learning which finger to lift. Five fingers prepare a move; four change its direction; the last turns a botched launch into a swing.

## 2 · Skeleton
**Entities:**
- five or six fingertips;
- six individually owned tentacles;
- the bell, the sediment, and elastic tension;
- anchors: a heavy rock, swaying kelp, a light reel, a playing film, the silt itself, and open water.

**Learning actions:** fan out, brace, load unevenly, peel one finger away, catch yourself, rake, haul.

**States of an arm:** slack, reaching (out of range: it stretches its last bit and drags the bell closer), latched (contact, then load), impending slip (shiver), slip, losing grip (interrupted), released.

**Boundary.** The five-finger moment is a voluntary burst of full-body play, and you come out of it one finger at a time.

**Invariant.** Once a finger owns an arm, it keeps that arm until release. Crossing, tumbling or lifting a neighbour never transfers it.

**Boredom threshold.** If releasing the fingers in different orders produced the same move, the full hand would become a boost button. Order must matter:
- every arm pulls at its own place on the rim, so a release changes the torque as well as the force;
- the last arm down becomes a hinge.

## 4 · Operation
**Five points of contact become one body with five commitments.**

**The three turns:**
1. **A request at one arm.** Your touch takes the arm that is under it, or the free arm that points most toward it.
2. **Acknowledgement before consequence.**
   - A tip that has something curls into a hook.
   - A tip that is only reaching stays open.
   - A ripple runs from the tip to the bell on contact.
3. **The bell's answer, which arrives later.**
   - The bell is never steered directly: it is dragged by every arm at its own rim point (force and torque), pushed by its own strokes (a double-tap or space), and sinks slightly.
   - So it bends out of shape between pulls, and it rolls through the opening a lifted finger leaves.

**Rules:**
- **Winch.** On a rock, kelp, the reel or the film, drawing your finger toward the jelly hauls the arm in; pushing it away pays out.
- **Water brace.** In open water the arm braces at your finger and follows it, but water is a weak hold that gives under load.
- **Silt.** In the silt, a shallow arm skids and rakes; a deep one catches like an edge.
- **Sag** (the "no time limit" fix). A loaded arm creeps, so a held pose changes while you decide.
- **Snap.** Lifting a loaded arm springs its rim, and the remaining arms throw the bell through the gap (an impulse along their pull, and a spin from their torque).
- **Slip.** An arm stretched three metres past its rest length lets go, with a pluck and a buzz. A shallow silt arm slips at two.

## 5 · Failures, and how each is designed out
- **The blind fist.** The posture glyph and bright, loaded arms read beyond the fingertips.
- **The unwanted launch.** An interrupted touch (`pointercancel`, a lost capture, the window losing focus) is not a release. The arm *loses its grip*: its tension fades over 0.4 s, with no snap.
- **The finger exam.** No finger is numbered for a task; the arm you take is the one your touch points to, and the posture shows which matters now.
- **The invisible refusal.** A finger out of reach still gets an answer: the arm stretches toward it, a dashed second ring appears, and the bell is drawn closer until the arm can latch.

## 6 · Change test
- **Unlimited spread.** Reach is 7.2 m. Beyond it the arm stretches and pleads, so you bring the bell around to help it.
- **Unlimited hold.** A loaded pose sags; the next second offers a different opportunity.
- **Instant mastery.** A perfect launch still has to land: the exit is a small ring above the far lip, and the floor is soft silt that bursts if you hit it bell-first.

## 7 · Choice
The pleasure chosen first is **Peel the jelly**, ending in **Catch the wreck**. The signature move is the last finger refusing to come along: the launch turns sideways around it, and you have four free fingers and a reason to put one back.

## 8 · The room (room one: the silt halfpipe)
- **The pipe:** bedrock in a parabola under eight slots of silt per column.
- **The buried gesture:** a hand (`cineosis-hand-butter`, 64 frames) is buried in the middle of the pipe. Its hesitation lies deepest, its reach above, its ending near the top, ordered along the floor so a sweep lifts them in sequence. Filler films lie elsewhere. The strata are tinted; buried frames glint in the section.
- **Raking:**
  - an arm dragged through the silt lifts frames as gaussian tiles of their own footage;
  - the tiles rise, turn and settle back where they land, as new deposits you can disturb again;
  - what you rake piles up a berm ahead of the arm;
  - hitting the silt bell-first bursts frames out.
- **The edit:** the frames you lift, in the order you lifted them, run along the foot of the screen and play in a monitor. Two people excavating the same shot get different films. The route through the silt becomes the edit.
- **One heavy anchor:** a rock pillar. **One loose reel:** light, so it comes when hauled and bounces off the bell. **Three kelp stalks** that sway with their anchors. **A film:** the buried hand, playing as a cloud of light, whose rim you can hook.
- **The exit** is a ring above the far lip. Reaching it reports the time, the moves you found (peel, the hinge swing, slingshot, cartwheel, caught the wreck, raked, bell-first into the silt) and the frames lifted, and seals the room.
- **Desktop:** hold 1–5 to plant fingers where the mouse is; the mouse is a sixth. Space strokes; R resets.
- **Getting there:** the Steer Lab is a place in the Open Water and on the index's Bonus shelf.

## 9 · Mapping
The hand talks in contacts; the jelly talks in shapes:
- **a hooked tip** says *I have something*;
- **a straightening, brightening arm** says *I'm taking load*;
- **a bead travelling to the bell** says *this is about to move you*;
- **a shiver and a red bead** say *I'm about to slip*;
- **a grey, limp arm** says *you can use me again*.

**Stress test (run in the build).** Five fingers down on kelp, silt and water; load; peel in a chosen order with one left down; re-plant on the rock during the tumble; rake the hand; cancel a touch. Results:
- the peel registered, with the hinge;
- the catch latched;
- the rake lifted nine frames of the buried hand;
- the cancel lost its grip without a launch.

**Not yet tested:** feel on real phones, and whether five-finger touches register reliably on the intended devices.

## 10 · Residue
There is a difference between watching a jelly cartwheel and feeling your little finger cause it. The first time, it should make you laugh; the second time, you should be able to do it on purpose.

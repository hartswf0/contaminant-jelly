# SCION SYSTEM INSTRUCTION: ROOMS AND PERFORMERS (three built worlds; the jellies perform for the camera)

**The direction:** "More in a library, data center, blockbuster ruin. Think of ways of worldbuilding with three-dimensional design, or making the jellyfish perform for the camera: more spirit, aliveness in each, more like rappers." Then: **"or bboys."**

**Nothing here was watched in motion or heard.** Stills only, from a slow software renderer.

## 1 · Three built worlds
Until now a "world" was a few objects in open water. These three are rooms: walls of furniture, a floor, their own lights. The orchestra performs in the aisle, and between close shots the camera shows the whole room down its length.

| world | what is built | how the music reaches the room | its experiment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **THE LIBRARY** (a reading room after hours) | eighteen bookcases in two rows, seven shelves each, every shelf a different run of spines; a long table; a tall mullioned window at the far end; three hanging lamps that light the wood | the lamps are the only light; the film specks read as dust in it | **Rule:** everyone whispers: every stroke is small and dim. **Swerve:** one animal performs at full size. **Test:** does it read as rude, or as the soloist? |
| **THE DATA CENTER** (a cold aisle) | eighteen racks, each with 120 status lights; a tiled metal floor; cable trays; four light bars | **every sound lights the racks of its own lane**, in that lane's colour; idle racks flicker | **Rule:** every sound lights its lane's racks. **Swerve:** one rack blinks red to no sound at all. **Test:** do you begin to hear a part that is not there? |
| **THE VIDEO STORE** (a rental store, long closed, half flooded) | wall shelving faced with film covers, one unit fallen; five low shelves, two toppled; about 500 tapes on the floor and shelves; a counter; fallen ceiling beams; a film of standing water; **a stack of five screens playing the film** | the screens are the playing film; their glow pulses on the beat | **Rule:** every screen shows the film that is playing. **Swerve:** one screen shows a different film. **Test:** which screen do you end up watching? |

- **The video store carries no brand.** "Blockbuster" was read as a kind of place, not a company: there is no logo, name or livery.
- Each has its own sound layer: page turns and a hush; fan hum, relay clicks and a low thud; tape hiss, mains hum and a machine clunk at each cut.
- There are now **fifteen experiments**; songs take them in turn.

## 2 · Performing for the camera
A new switch on the card: **TO CAMERA**, **CYPHER**, or no performance. It applies in every world except the record, which is looked down on.

**What every soloist now does (the "aliveness"):**
| | |
|---|---|
| **anticipation** | just before its sound it dips and squashes. The page knows the next event, so the animal can gather itself |
| **stretch** | on the stroke it lengthens along its axis and narrows |
| **the lens** | it leans its bell toward the camera, wherever the camera is |
| **the nod** | it dips on every beat |

**TO CAMERA (like an MC):**
- **One animal at a time is on.** Every two bars the one that has been sounding most takes over. The one who just went is passed over, so turns rotate. Hats and ticks rarely get a turn.
- **It comes downstage** to a mark near the camera, grows, leans hard into the lens, and steps side to side on the beat.
- **Its section is its crew:** they bounce behind it on beats two and four.
- **Everyone else steps back and dims.**
- **The camera works for it:** a wide shot, then low and close with a wider lens and a tilted horizon, then from the side, then from above its shoulder.

**CYPHER (like b-boys):**
- **The soloists form a ring.** One enters for two bars.
- **Bar one, toprock:** side to side on each beat, leaning into each step.
- **Three beats of windmill:** its axis is laid almost flat and swung round, while it circles low.
- **The last beat, a freeze:** upside down, held still and bright. Every moon jelly flares, the lens snaps in tight, and a low hit sounds.
- **The camera** circles the ring from below, goes in close, or looks straight down into it.

The data center and the video store open in CYPHER; the library and the others open TO CAMERA.

## 3 · Checked
| check | result |
|---|---|
| all fifteen experiments | no page or shader errors on the ones shot: the three new worlds, Deep Water, the Measured Bay |
| the rooms | each reads as a room in a wide still: shelves and a lamp; racks and their lights; shelves, tapes and screens |
| performing | stills show an animal close to the lens and leaning into it, and, in the cypher, one tilted over in the middle of a ring |
| **repaired after the first stills** | film specks near the lens swelled into white blobs: their size is now capped and they fade close up. The close shots hid the rooms: every built room now gets a wide shot down its aisle. The card covered a third of the picture: it is narrower and folds itself after fourteen seconds unless you touch it |

**Not checked:**
- **Any of the motion:** the nod, the anticipation, the toprock, the windmill, the freeze, the crew's bounce. These only exist in time.
- **Whether a jellyfish laid on its side reads as a windmill** or as a mistake.
- **Speed.** The rooms add lights and a few hundred objects; the tapes are halved on a phone, nothing else is.
- **The other ten experiments** were not re-shot after these changes.

## 4 · Considered, not built
| idea | why not yet |
|---|---|
| **The camera as a character:** handheld shake on the kick, a whip-pan to whoever comes in | wants watching in motion first |
| **A crowd that reacts:** the ring leaning in on the windmill, backing off after the freeze | the flare at the freeze is the first step |
| **Battles:** two sections facing each other, trading two bars | needs a song with two clear voices; several swarm songs have them |
| **Rooms that keep the damage:** tapes knocked off shelves by a stroke, pages lifted in the library | needs each object to be simulated |
| **More rooms:** a projection booth, a server hall seen from the cable tray, a flooded stack of card catalogues | each is an afternoon of building |

The Colloquy of Mobiles
1968 · reconstructed 2018 · simulated here
Gordon Pask (Systems Research Ltd.), with sculpture by Yolanda Sonnabend and engineering by Marc Dowson and Tony Watts.
First shown in Cybernetic Serendipity, Institute of Contemporary Arts, London, 2 August – 20 October 1968. Full-scale reconstruction by Paul Pangaro and TJ McLeish (2018), now in the collection of ZKM | Center for Art and Media Karlsruhe.

Five mobiles hang from a triangular frame and talk to one another in light and sound. Each carries two drives, O (orange) and P (puce), that rise for as long as they go unmet. None can satisfy itself. A “male” is relieved only when a “female” turns his own beam back onto the right vane with her mirror, and she is relieved only by hearing that it worked.

Out of these trades the five learn. Each female remembers which way to tilt for each male, so a second meeting is quicker than the first. Two males share one bar and must agree, by a majority vote weighted by need, whether it turns or stops.

Pask wanted an “aesthetically potent environment”, one that lets the visitor in. Here, as at the ICA, you can join the conversation. Shine a torch at a female and you take the male’s part. Hold up a mirror and you take hers. Stand in a beam and you break a conversation. Push the bar and you compose them all.

The gender personas are Pask and Sonnabend’s, a deliberate period satire of 1960s courtship clichés built into who must signal and who must reflect. They are kept here as historical labels, not as a claim about anyone.

This version runs the machine as five asynchronous state programs, the same for a given seed. Each mobile is played by a jelly from this site: G the Sentinel, J the Celluloid, F1 the Glass Moon, F2 the Afterlight Medusa, F3 the Fleck Veil. Its pulse comes from its state. Where the sources leave gaps, the choices are marked as interpretation in the code.

NOTATION ×

G, J
the two “male” mobiles at the ends of bar Z, each turning 180° on its own axis (played by the Sentinel and the Celluloid)
Z
the bar that carries them through 360°; it halts or turns by majority decision, weighted by drive
F1–F3
the three “female” mobiles near the frame’s outer vertices, each turning through a bounded 90° arc between limit switches
O, P
the two drives, orange and puce: μ ← μ + δ while unmet; μ ← μ − ω on satisfaction
Lmin, Lmax
thresholds of the four regimes: satisfied (both below Lmin), O, P, or dual (both above Lmax)
A
the male’s display: his two drives as lit columns
u
his flashing lamp, frequency-coded for who he is and which drive he wants satisfied
B
his steady, energetic beam: the costly commitment of stage 4
C, D
his reinforcement vanes, C for O (up), D for P (down); a reflection on the right one sets F = 1
a
her aperture and photocell, which catches u
b
her servo mirror, tilted up for O, down for P
tone
her emitter, phase-locked to his flashes; his sound sensor listens for it
MA, MB
her memories, per male, of the mirror angle that last satisfied his O (MA) or P (MB)
τ
the window for her mirror search; if no reinforcement lands within it, both abort and scan again
hue
each mobile’s own colour, on its halo and filament: G red, J green, F1 blue, F2 violet, F3 pale gold; its brightness is its need
1–7
scan · contact · phase-lock · commitment · mirror sweep · closure (F = 1) · consolidate and resume

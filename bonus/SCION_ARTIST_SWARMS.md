# SCION SYSTEM INSTRUCTION: THE ARTIST SWARMS (38 songs from one crate, and an orchestra of jellyfish to perform them)

**The direction:**
- "Make a song with each swarm and make better mixes with more samples, make more with less" (with the archive *SCION Artist Swarms*: 38 production prompts and a router).
- "For each song make a real jelly choreography music video to the music."
- "Many jellyfish dancing, each a sound or a sample, singing jellyfish, beautiful orchestra, symphony, museum quality. We have Real Jelly and many types of jelly. Each of the songs gets its own."

**Nothing here was heard.** This machine cannot play audio. Every song is a rendered proposal, not an accepted passage; every listening test the prompts ask for is still to be done.

## 1 · How the three phrases were read (defaults, all reversible)
| the phrase | what was built |
|---|---|
| **a song with each swarm** | 38 songs, one per prompt, each built round that prompt's mechanism, with its own control version |
| **more samples** | every sound in all 38 is a cut from your catalog. **No synthesis at all:** drums, bass, leads, voices and textures are all sampled |
| **more with less** | one shared crate of **53 cuts** makes all 38 songs (49 were used, from 31 recordings). A song uses 3 to 11 of them. A sound is cut, tuned, filtered and placed, never re-recorded |
| **better mixes** | see section 4 |
| **a music video for each** | **The Jelly Orchestra:** a live scene, not a video file. Each song has its own cast of jellyfish, formation and cuts (section 5) |

**The artist names in the prompts are lenses only.** Nothing imitates a recording or an artist. The songs carry the prompts' own experiment titles, and no artist name appears in any label.

### Substitutions (the prompts ask for things I could not supply)
- **No spoken words were recorded.** Eleven prompts ask for an original spoken line (07, 09, 15, 17, 26, 30, 31, 33, 34, 35, 38). A choir phrase or a grunt from the catalog stands in as "the voice". Whether any of them is words was not heard, so tests about meaning or intelligibility cannot be run on these.
- **No field recording** (12): a catalog texture stands in, and its events are measured, not heard.
- **No synth tone** (13, 14): a short slice of a sample stands in.
- **Third branches were not built** where a prompt asks for three (02 random jitter, 15 reply absent, 25 voice removed, 35 silence).
- **Shorter than asked:** 08 asks for three one-minute renders; the three seeds are eight bars each.

## 4 · The mixes: what changed, and what was measured
| change | why |
|---|---|
| **Every clip is levelled when it enters the crate** (hits to the same peak, held sounds to the same average) | earlier leads sat 5 to 15 dB under the drums by accident |
| **Each kind of sound has its own band:** leads, voices and textures are cut below 120 to 200 Hz; the found hat is cut below 3 kHz; sampled bass is kept below 500 Hz | the low end belongs to the kick and the bass only |
| **Sampled bass, tuned:** a low catalog note is re-pitched to each song's root | no synthetic sub |
| **Pitched clips are tuned to each other** (within 2.5 semitones, to a third, fourth, fifth or sixth of the lead) | chosen sounds no longer clash by accident |
| **One room for everything:** each sound sends some of itself to the same room | sounds from different recordings share a space |
| **A master limiter:** one gain into a soft curve | the songs are about 3 dB louder for the same peak, and all at one level |

**Measured across the 38:**
| | |
|---|---|
| average level | -15.5 dB in 33 songs; -16.0 to -17.5 in five sparse ones |
| lead against drums | the lead is 2 dB above the drums at the median (before: 3 to 8 dB below) |
| peaks | under -0.5 dBFS; no clipping |
| clicks | largest step at any edit: 10% of full scale |
| mono | 0 to 1.7 dB lost in 36 songs; **3.7 dB in song 08**, which is mostly room |
| low end | 0 to 82% of the energy, by song; **above 70% in 04, 19, 22 and 28**, which may be bottom-heavy |
| the instrument plays what was rendered | all 38: its own render is 43 to 83 dB from the reference |

**Stems and the master.** The limiter is not a plain gain, so stems no longer add up to the master. The stems are the lanes before the limiter; the master is the limiter applied to their sum. The studio's RENDER reproduces both.

**A false positive caught:** a number of crate clips read as "A sharp, 918.8 Hz". That is the edge of my pitch search, not a pitch. Those clips are treated as unpitched and are never tuned.

## 5 · The Jelly Orchestra
**What it is:** dark water, light from above, marine snow, and a cast of jellyfish. **Each jellyfish is one sound of the song.** When its sound plays it squeezes, brightens, and is pushed upward by the stroke; then it drifts back to its place.

| kind of sound | species |
|---|---|
| kick | The Sentinel |
| snare | The Celluloid |
| hat, tick | The Doodle Jelly (small) |
| bass | The Sewer Creature (large) |
| choir | The Glass Moon |
| phrase | The Golden Splat |
| glide | The Film-Reel Jelly |
| call, breath | The Afterlight Medusa |
| tail, hole | The Fleck Veil |
| bed, texture | The Film Bell |

**Each song gets its own:**
- **cast:** its own sounds, up to eleven animals (seven on a phone), sized by role;
- **formation:** one of six (a ring, an arc like choir risers, two rooms, a rising spiral, a V, a procession), which breathes every four bars and turns every eight;
- **cuts:** five camera positions, changing every four bars; one of them follows whichever animal is sounding;
- **water:** its own colour;
- **placard:** number, title, mechanism, tempo, how many animals and recordings.

It plays on from song to song (PLAY ON), or choose from the menu. It also plays the eight album pieces and the earlier tracks: 50 in all.

**Tested:** three songs loaded with the right cast and no errors; animals fired on their sounds. **Not tested:** the other 35 in the orchestra page, and how it runs on a phone.

### The Real Jelly, and why it is a soloist
The Real Jelly is a simulated soft body. Many at once would not run, so the orchestra uses the ten lighter species. The Real Jelly has a **solo**: `realjelly.html?song=<id>` (the SOLO link in the orchestra) plays a song while every kick fires one of its pacemakers.

**The solo could not be verified here.** In this machine's software renderer the animal's nerve clock ran twenty times too slow (1.2 s in 25 s), so its muscle was still recovering when each kick came. The code loads and runs without errors; whether the animal keeps up with the kicks needs a real device.

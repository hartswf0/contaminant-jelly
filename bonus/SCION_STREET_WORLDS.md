# SCION SYSTEM INSTRUCTION · THE STREET AND THE WORLDS WALL

Where:
- The wall: `bonus/worlds.html` (Bolas edition: `/bolas/worlds/`), and a Worlds section on the Bolas front page.
- The street worlds: `bonus/jellyorchestra.html?look=stencil`, `poster`, `decollage`, `spray`, `pasteup`.
- The street over a city: `?city=b5&lens=stencil` (or `poster`, `decollage`, `spray`).

## 1. Thesis
The orchestra has been shown as water, light and rooms. The reference boards ask for a fourth thing: a wall.
Paint is flat, has edges, runs, and is pasted over and torn. So the street worlds do not light the orchestra.
They print it.

## 2. The five street worlds
Each is a rule for turning light into paint. All sit on generated concrete (stains, pores) and share one
behaviour: a heavy mark runs downward.

| World | Rule | Hand-made gesture | Test | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STENCIL | Two sheets: black for what is lit, red for what is brightest, on a rolled cream ground | Speckled sprayed edges, runs | With two colours, can you still tell who is playing? | Too coarse for music |
| CUT POSTER | Three flat colours, red on top; nothing is shaded | Edges break into blocks; the wall shows through in patches | Does a poster that moves stay a poster? | Becomes a cartoon |
| DÉCOLLAGE | Overlapping sheets, each printed its own way (six inks, halftone or flat); some torn back to the wall | White torn rims; an animal changes ink when it crosses a tear | Do you follow animals across the tears? | The scraps win and the orchestra becomes a wall |
| SPRAY WALL | A dark wall; each mark in its own colour with a pale outline and mist of the same colour | Mist, speckle, runs, a short trail | Is the still animal the tag you remember? | White mush |
| PASTE-UP | Prints from the film pasted on the wall; the orchestra cut out and pasted over them | White paper core at every cut edge, a small cast shadow, three stops of dye, one hatch over every cut-out | Can you tell cut-outs from prints at a glance? | The white edge does nothing |

Each has its own swerve on the card (one animal late, contrary, left out, or still).

## 3. Street builders (in a city with cut lists)
| Lens | Builder |
|---|---|
| stencil | Each frame is sprayed onto its tower, a little crooked every time |
| poster | Frames are pasted beside the tower in rows |
| décollage | A frame is torn off its source, which stays bare; the cut is made of what the city lost |
| spray | The route from source to cut is sprayed across the city and left to fade |
| paste-up | Each frame is cut from its source print and pasted on the cut's tower with its white edge showing |

That makes seventeen builders. Lenses are now chosen by name (`&lens=stencil`); numbers still work.

## 4. The worlds wall
- One page, styled as a concrete wall of pasted sheets.
- Sections: the street, experiments, rooms, celluloid cities, builders, the brief.
- Every tile is a still captured from the running page, with the world's rule under it, and opens that world.
- The header image and the three boards under "the brief" are the reference boards supplied for this work. The
  page says so, and says the running worlds do not match them yet.

## 5. What was checked
- Headless stills of all four street worlds alone, and of all four over ALLWAYS · ARGON: no page errors.
- Stills of every world and of the seven Bolas cities were captured for the wall.
- The wall page was opened at desktop and phone width.

## 6. What was not checked
- I cannot watch motion or hear sound. Whether paint that moves reads as paint is yours to judge.
- Over a city, the test stills landed on the wide overhead shot, so the city is small in frame and the paint is
  sparse. Closer shots were not captured.
- The street builders share code with paper, card and phosphor; they were not run for a full cut.
- The live sites and phone speed were not tested.

## 7. Distance from the boards
The boards have things the running worlds do not: drawn jellyfish with ink texture, film strips as towers with
pictures in every frame, lettering, peeling paper with shadow. The running worlds only re-colour what the
orchestra already renders. Closing that gap would mean painted geometry (pasted sheets as objects in the scene,
towers textured as wheat-paste), not more filters.

## 8. Second pass: colour, legibility, collage after paeleo
What `paeleo/handmade` does (Ernst 03, Gilliam ephemera 52) and what was taken from it:
- **A pasted thing keeps the white core of its paper at the scissor edge and casts a small shadow.** Every lit
  thing in décollage, spray and paste-up now has that edge; paste-up also has the shadow.
- **Margin shown means it came from somewhere else.** The paste-up builder uses this as its rule.
- **Prints are toned with a short dye ramp and an uneven wash.** Paste-up scraps are real frames from the film the
  orchestra swims through, toned in the scrap's two colours with a wash. Décollage scraps carry a faint halftone of
  the same film, so no scrap is a dead flat rectangle.
- **One line language.** Every paste-up cut-out is hatched the same way.

Other changes:
- The film specks are hidden on the street. Each one was being stencilled and outlined, and the wall turned to confetti.
- Poster: cream stock, teal, red, and a black keyline round every shape.
- Scraps are no longer a grid. The first version cut the wall into a checkerboard, which was wrong: a wall of posters
  has no rows. Now there are seven layers of sheets, each layer at its own angle and scale, each sheet its own size and
  tilt; upper sheets cover the lower ones and cast a shadow on them, and the wall shows where no sheet landed.
- On pale worlds the title and the marker label sit on a dark plate.
- Fixed: switching from one city to another could throw an error for a moment (moon jellies kept a floor number
  from the old city).

Not taken from paeleo: its rights ledger per fragment, and cut-outs as separate objects with pins. Here the collage
is still made by a filter over one rendered picture, which is why film strips get a paper edge too.

## 9. Third pass: the wall is worked on while the song plays
A collage that never changes is a texture. In the paeleo threshold films (the scene that ends "come in") the pieces
change for a reason and on a measured rhythm. What was read, and what was taken:

| Source | What it does | What the wall now does |
|---|---|---|
| 04 Mouris, image torrent | The rate of replacement is the scene's pressure: frantic under the call, held when the dog lies down | Loud bars: the nervous sheets turn over every 2 beats. Quiet bars: sheets hold for 8 or 16 beats |
| 51 Mouris montage | Whole-image substitution on a cutting score, each held a measured number of frames | Each sheet has its own period and phase in beats; a change is a whole new print and ink |
| 43 Collage graph | Typed rewrites: ENTER, EXIT, REPLACE, each caused by a score event | ENTER: a sheet is pasted on, the brush crossing it. REPLACE: a new print is pasted over the old. EXIT: the sheet is torn off |
| 52 Gilliam ephemera | Shot on twos; held frames | The wall's clock is stepped at 12 a second |
| Décollage itself | Tearing leaves residue | A torn-off sheet leaves white scraps at its edges for as long as it is gone |

- A paste or a tear takes a beat and a half, moves in the sheet's own direction, and has a ragged white front.
- About one sheet in five is absent in any generation, so the bare wall moves around too.
- The song's bar loudness (three levels) is the cutting score. Nothing is random per frame: the same song gives the
  same wall.

Checked: six stills each of décollage and paste-up between 13 s and 34 s of song 27. The sheets, prints and inks
differ from still to still, and there were no page errors.
Not checked: the motion itself. I cannot see whether a paste reads as a brush stroke, a tear as a tear, or whether
the turnover in loud bars is exciting or just busy. Not taken: MERGE and SPLIT rules, and pieces that belong to an
actor (in paeleo the dog is made of pieces; here the jellies are still rendered whole and cut out).

## 10. Fourth pass: captured, not stuck on
The earlier passes laid the live jellies over the paper as cut-outs with a white edge. That is a sticker: the paper
and the picture have nothing to do with each other. Now the paper is made from the picture.

- **Capture.** Once a beat the live camera picture is kept: 16 frames at half size, four bars of memory.
- **Print.** A sheet shows the frame taken at the beat it was pasted, at the sheet's own place in the view (with a
  small shift and enlargement, as a hand-placed print would have). Décollage prints it in the sheet's two inks,
  halftone or flat. Paste-up exposes it in its own colours onto a toned frame of the film.
- **Live, then set.** A sheet is live for its first beat, like a viewfinder. Then it sets and never moves again.
- **Collage.** The wall is therefore one view at many moments: the same animal appears on several sheets, where it
  was at each of their beats.
- **Décollage.** Tearing a sheet off uncovers an older moment underneath, or bare wall.
- **Nothing is laid on top.** The cut-out layer, the white core and the cast shadow are gone from these two worlds.
  The bare wall shows no animals at all.
- Sheet periods are now 2, 4 or 8 beats, so no sheet outlives the memory.

Checked: six stills of each world between 13 s and 33 s; about 49 captures had been taken by then; prints of jellies
and film strips are recognisable inside the sheets and differ from sheet to sheet; no page errors.
Not checked: the motion, and the moment a live sheet sets. On a phone the capture buffer is at most 4096 px wide;
its cost there was not measured.

## 11. Fifth pass: watchable, with footage behind
The fourth pass was unwatchable: seven layers of small sheets, changing every beat, with nothing whole to look at.
The bar in every paeleo swarm prompt is "a finished, watchable work", and the ARAP swarm's theorem is to change a
thing while keeping it recognisable. Measured against that, the wall failed. What changed:

**Collage (décollage and paste-up)**
- The live picture is whole and readable underneath. Décollage prints it in one ink on cream; paste-up plays it in
  its own colours.
- Over it, a few large sheets (three layers, not seven; under half of the places filled), each still a frame the
  camera took at the beat it was pasted, live for one beat and then set.
- Slow: a sheet lasts 8 or 12 beats, 4 only for a few sheets in the loudest bars; a paste or a tear takes two beats.
- Décollage uses three ink pairs, not six. No residue scraps.
- Paste-up prints are photographs with a white border on the wall's own ground, and they yellow as they age.

**Footage behind**
- Street worlds: the song's film now plays on the wall itself as a faded mural, under the paint.
- Bolas cities: the city now plays over the actual film file. The film of the cut the first school is rebuilding
  (or, in a city without cut lists, a film on the current street) plays as video behind the city, and a caption
  names it. All seven Bolas cities have local files for their films.
- The fourteen poem cities do not: their sources are pages on an outside archive, not files this site holds.

Checked: stills of décollage, paste-up and stencil over 20 s (the picture is readable, the mural shows); in
ALLWAYS · ARGON and CRASH LAB the video element was playing (its clock advanced) and the film is visible behind the
towers; no page errors.
Not checked: motion, sound, phone speed with a video decoding behind the scene, the live site. The city video is
muted; the song is the sound.

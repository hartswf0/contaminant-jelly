Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Two shots, one hard cut, locked-off camera in both.

Shot 1 (0-5.5s), Turkey's seat, locked-off camera, the camera never moves: exactly the cockpit reference for the whole shot, riveted steel tin, centered brass six-bolt porthole with a chip at two o'clock, empty ballast slot lower left, chalk chart lower center, brass lamp toggle lower right, amber lamp overhead, dent in the right wall, all in the house watercolour. From the first frame the porthole reference is in the glass, drawn like an old steel engraving: a round stone culvert ring, a row of upright iron bars and tied-back drapes as flat black cut-out layers shaded only by fine ruled parallel lines, stacked like a paper toy theatre. At 1s only a feathered wing tip enters at the lower right corner, flips the toggle and leaves. The water behind the bars lights to a teal glow, so everything is a backlit silhouette. The two drapes slide a little apart, flat and rigid, and one black trout silhouette glides sideways behind the bars.

Shot 2 (5.5-8s), Trout's side, hard cut, locked-off camera, the camera never moves: exactly the outside reference, held still, the tin on the riverbed, the port in the same place, Turkey small behind the amber glass. In the left foreground one speckled trout floats beside the engraved stone ring, whose rivet-studded bars stand like a row of teeth. One pale hard-edged wedge of light runs from the port through the bars, flickering, throwing bar shadows on the silt.

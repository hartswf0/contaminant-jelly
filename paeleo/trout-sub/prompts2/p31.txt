# feedstock: something-in-the-kitchen | control: lamp | atlas: a43
Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Two shots, one hard cut, locked-off camera in both.

Shot 1 (0-5.5s), Turkey's seat, locked-off camera, the camera never moves: exactly the cockpit reference throughout in the house watercolour: steel tin, brass six-bolt porthole chipped at two o'clock, ballast slot lower left, chalk chart, lamp toggle lower right, dent on the right. From the first frame the porthole reference is in the glass, looking like a photographed miniature, soft grain, faded colour: a real dented steel tin, riveted like this hull, tipped on its side, large at the left, open mouth toward us, stamped 375°F, four silver minnows inside; cool white light from above, violet dusk far behind. At 1s only a feathered wing tip enters at the lower right corner, flips the toggle and leaves. The inside of the tin then glows warm amber, and the warmth stays low: amber water pools on the tin floor under a flat lid of cold blue water; each bubble rises, stops at that lid and slides sideways. The minnows hold still; one blinks.

Shot 2 (5.5-8s), Trout's side, hard cut, locked-off camera, the camera never moves: exactly the outside reference, held still, the long tin on the riverbed, Turkey small behind the amber port. In the left foreground a speckled trout beside the same small tipped tin, minnows basking in its amber glow. The small tin lifts just off the gravel and two minnows pop straight up out of its mouth. The trout only stares.

Light as in the last reference: cool from above, violet behind; no people, no corridor. The only lettering is 375°F on the small tin; no other text, no labels, no captions.

Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Two shots, one hard cut, locked-off camera in both.

Shot 1 (0-5.5s), Turkey's seat, locked-off camera, the camera never moves: exactly the cockpit reference throughout in the house watercolour: steel tin, brass six-bolt porthole chipped at two o'clock, ballast slot lower left, chalk chart, lamp toggle lower right, dent on the right. From the first frame the porthole reference is in the glass, but looking like a photographed miniature room with soft grain and faded colour: a diner serving window lit by cool white panels overhead, violet dusk behind, a speckled trout cook in a white chef hat large at the left edge holding one long silver baster, two small trout customers deep behind at the counter, a grid of small colour squares on the back wall. At 1s only a feathered wing tip enters at the lower left corner, drops an iron weight into the slot and leaves. The cook, deadpan, squeezes the baster once over a dish on the sill, then slowly turns the same baster to point straight at us and squeezes: a fan of bubbles spreads on the glass. The cook blinks.

Shot 2 (5.5-8s), Trout's side, hard cut, locked-off camera, the camera never moves: exactly the outside reference, held still, the tin on the riverbed, Turkey small behind the amber port. In the left foreground the trout cook beside a plain trout, the silver baster held between them at arm's length. The cook dribbles it once along the tin's rim, then taps one rivet with its tip and listens.

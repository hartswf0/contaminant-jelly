# feedstock: mind-game-trailer | control: ballast | atlas: a24,a25
Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images.

Shot 1 (0-5.5s), Turkey's seat, locked-off camera, the camera never moves: exactly the cockpit reference throughout, in the house watercolour: steel tin, brass six-bolt porthole chipped at two o'clock, ballast slot lower left, chalk chart, lamp toggle lower right, dent on the right. From the first frame the glass holds the porthole reference as a grainy grey-blue photographed cutout with a thin loose outline, like an old archive print: a dented tin mailbox painted "#44" on a steeply leaning post, door open, a bulging canvas sack spilling wax-sealed envelopes; beside it a speckled trout in a small postal cap. At 1s only a wing tip enters lower left, drops a small iron weight into the slot and leaves. One envelope slides out and the trout takes it in its mouth: in that single frame box, sack and trout snap into a flat drawing of angular ice-blue and cobalt facets in broken pencil, the water straight white streaks; then snap back. The sack sags limp like a bag of washing.

Shot 2 (5.5-8s), Trout's side, hard cut, locked-off camera, the camera never moves: exactly the outside reference, held still: the tin on the riverbed, Turkey small behind the amber port. The "#44" box and limp sack stand at the left edge as the grey photograph. The same capped trout, envelope in mouth, holds the envelope up to the port, snapping to facets and back at that instant. Loose envelopes slide along the gravel and vanish under the tin as if sucked in.

The snap is a cut, never a fade; no human faces. The only lettering is "#44" on the mailbox; no other text, no labels, no captions.

Tourkey Trouttown · hand films · batch 1

Made by hand

Each porthole gets a short film in which the thing is made on a table, then seen by Turkey through the glass, then by the trout from the river. The rule for all of them: handmake or forage, no style costumes. Everything on screen is either real printed matter from open archives, cut and hinged, or the actual object built in code.

Every film was made by one agent and then repaired by a second who had not seen it made. Their reports, the sources and the scripts are all here.

Porthole 01 · Bow · 31 s

Giant Size 11 Rubber Boot blocking beam

Nearly passed

Turkey looking out: Shipwreck, giant’s foot, or tunnel?
Trout looking in: Boot-sole flashlight or toe-biting crab?
Rule: Silhouette (SCION 02): backlit shadow
Descends from: Monty Python (Gilliam, 1969)

Foraged from Wikimedia Commons, all public domain or CC0: the boot is cut from a Boston Rubber Shoe Co. poster (Missouri Historical Society via DPLA); trout from Denton's 1896 plate; stones from an Everdingen etching (NGA); Turkey's head from Audubon; scissors from an 1880 Worman cut. Handmade: bread-roll tin, brass porthole, pins, tissue water, weeds. SCION 01 cutout: faceted cuts, held twos, the hole left in the poster. SCION 02 shadow theatre: lamp under the glass, a pinned ankle with a limit. SCION 45 occlusion: the beam's shadow is computed from the paper standing in it. Weak: the crab reading leans on its caption; sound is synthesized. Critic: pulled the porthole view back so the whole foot, heel arch and ankle sit in the glass (it was an unnameable black blob); pinned handmade tinplate bread tongs to the tin's bow that snap three times at the lifted toe, with a click, so the crab reading no longer rests on the caption; raised the poster in part 1 so the caption no longer covers the sole being cut. Still weak: the tunnel reading is only the small lit gap under the heel arch; the glass is empty for about 2.5 s before the boot drops; the tongs slide in already attached rather than being cut and pinned on screen; sound is synthesized.

Direction given to the maker

Lineage is comic cutout collage. Forage public-domain engravings (a rubber boot or wellington from a trade catalogue or encyclopaedia plate, a trout from a natural-history plate, river-bed stones) from Wikimedia Commons, cut them out, and assemble the giant boot on a table with visible scissors-cut edges and drop shadows, hinged at the ankle. The boot blocks an amber lamp beam.

What the maker reported

Method. Comic cutout collage on a light table, shot on twos. Part 1 (0-11 s, 35%): scissors cut the boot out of a rubber-boot poster along a faceted path, the boot lifts out leaving a real hole, the sheet is pulled away, the ankle is snipped and pinned with a brass fastener, the foot wags on its hinge, then tissue water, etched stones, weeds, the tin, its porthole ring and the trout slide in; the top light fades and the lamp under the glass comes on. Part 2 (11-21 s): through the six-bolt brass porthole, close on the toe only, the boot falls into the amber beam and blocks it while Turkey's lamp sweeps; caption "Turkey: Shipwreck? Giant's foot? Or a tunnel?". Part 3 (21-31 s): river side, tin and porthole in frame, the boot lifts twice so the beam floods out under the sole toward the trout while the tin scuttles and nips at the toe; caption "Trout: Is its sole a torch, or is a crab biting its toe?". The Silhouette rule is shown by the same paper pieces turning into backlit shadows, with the beam's shadow computed from whatever stands in it. The scene is laid out mirrored (tin on the right) so the boot is never flipped and its poster lettering stays readable. Sound is synthesized with Mix. Five stills rounds plus a contact sheet from the final mp4. Spinning black-and-white spiral word disc

Nearly passed

Turkey looking out: Whirlpool turbine or hypnosis trap?
Trout looking in: Diner pie-prize wheel or washing machine?
Rule: Rotating Type & Depth (Oucipo)
Descends from: Marcel Duchamp: Anémic cinéma (1926)

Handmade in code: a card disc on an iron wheel, inked with one spiral band by a clamped brush as the wheel turns, then three eccentric rings (disc re-pinned off centre each time); cut-paper letters set singly from both ends inward; twelve brass tacks. Direction given to the maker

Lineage is a spinning optical disc with words set in a spiral. Handmade: typeset your own original spiral sentence about the river (not any existing text) on a black and white disc, show the disc being inked ring by ring and the letters set one at a time, then spin it at speeds where the spiral seems to advance and recede. The rule is a spiral palindrome if you can manage one; say honestly in the note if it is only near.

What the maker reported

Method. Handmade spinning lettered disc, 34 s, 960x540, 24 fps, 2.4 MB. Part 1 (0 to 15.3 s, the making, on an overhead bench): a bare card disc on an iron banding wheel; a flat brush clamped on a steel rule inks one 1.5-turn spiral band while the wheel turns under it (wet sheen, bristle streaks, the brush running dry at the hub end); the brush is swapped for a ruling pen and three eccentric rings are inked ring by ring, the disc re-pinned off centre for each (pinholes stay); 31 cut-paper pieces are lifted off a baize galley by a waxed stick and set one at a time from both ends of the spiral toward the middle; twelve brass tacks go round the rim; a flick starts it spinning. The sentence is my own and is an exact letter palindrome, spaces included (asserted in code): NOW TRAP A POOL · ROTOR · LOOP A PART WON. Part 2 (15.3 to 24.7 s): through Turkey's six-bolt brass porthole the disc turns anticlockwise at 0.5, 1 and 2 turns a second so the spiral drains toward the hub, silt spirals in and a trout is drawn nose-first toward it, then it reverses and the spiral pours outward; caption asks whirlpool turbine or hypnosis trap. Part 3 (24.7 to 34 s): river side with the tin and its porthole in frame; the disc spins clockwise up to 2 turns a second (froth thrown off the rim, a strand of weed whirled round like laundry), then runs down under friction with a sprung reed ticking on the tacks and comes to rest with the reed on the middle T of ROTOR; caption asks pie-prize wheel or washing machine. Rule (Rotating Type and Depth): only rotation is drawn; motion blur is the real average of 8 shutter samples per frame, so at speed the letters dissolve and the bare spiral advances or recedes, and at exactly 2 turns a second the twelve tacks stand still against 24 fps (asserted). Sound is synthesized: brush, pen, a note per letter kind so the pairs sound twice, tack taps, a rotor hum that follows the spin, reed ticks at the computed tack crossings, a bell at the stop. Five rounds of contact sheets; fixes included enlarging the rings, de-mossing the legs, rebuilding the caught weed, recomposing the porthole so the trout is inside the glass, smoother shutter blur, and aligning the tack ring so the wheel stops on the middle T.

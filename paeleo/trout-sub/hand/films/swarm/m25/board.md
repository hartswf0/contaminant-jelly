## What is special about this sheet

**Nobody on this sheet used the rubber.** There is no rubbed patch and no ghost word anywhere on either side.
When M. is unhappy with a letter he goes over it harder. So this film has no `erase` pose and no `rub_mark`: its
corrections are pressure. Do not add a rubbing-out because the other making films have one.

**The rule of the porthole is inversion, and so are the traces.** A helmet that holds water in; a letter gone
over three times; a wild surprise that is the opposite of the river ("it is not wet"); a sheet that reaches each
friend the wrong way round. The camera's turn-overs (the dive that spins, the 180 degree turn, the swing round in
the river) are the same idea the children were working on.

## A warning about the photographs

A broad shadow lies over the right-hand third of the front photograph and the top left of the back one.
**Compare pressure only between marks that sit side by side in the same light**: the fish against the bowl loops
it lies on; the three reworked b's against the words beside them; K.'s line against N.'s across the gutter. Do
not stage a line that fades along its length.

## What the film is

M. is asked what Turkey sees and draws the thing on the slip: a bowl, and then, pressed hard, a whole fish across
it. Asked what the trout think, he turns the sheet over and borrows the trout's own plate. K. gets the page upside
down, turns it, goes down through the ring into the tin, turns round and looks out, and comes back with three
small sure lines that end up at the opposite of wet. N. presses a button until it is black, goes out through the
glass, swings round in the river, sees fish beside a giant suit, writes it enormous until the paper stops, and
then sits with his pencil up over the next line. The room goes on round him.

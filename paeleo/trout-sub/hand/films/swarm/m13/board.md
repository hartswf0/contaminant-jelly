## A warning about the photographs before the traces

The left third of the front photograph lies in the photographer's shadow, so words at the left of each line look
darker than words at the right. **Pressure along a line cannot be read from this photograph** and the film must
not stage "pressing hard, then fading". The only pressure differences used below are ones that hold inside the
same lighting: the two faint words on line 2, the rubbed-out ghosts in the ring, the rubbed-out first name.

## What the film is

The pilot is asked what Turkey sees and answers by speaking as a trout. The first friend is asked to sit in
Turkey's seat and does: her endings look out and never mention him. The next friend is asked to be the trout
looking back, and the page stays white. The camera takes each journey; only two of them arrive.

**The porthole's rule, Clockwise Etiquette, is kept by the camera:** the sheet is passed left as the page says
("Pass your paper left"), which round a table seen from above is clockwise; every `turn` goes to the right
(`direction=1`); a page that arrives turned is squared up clockwise (`rot` only ever increases); dives spin
clockwise.

Times given for the class film's staged endings (`c13/scene_turkey`) are c13 FILM times; that module's own clock
is 20.0 s less (so "about 25 s" is `draw(5.0, ink)`). `c13/scene_pilot` starts at 0, so its times are its own.

**Captions:** `reads` spelling, once each, when the line is finished. Six in all. Nothing else is captioned.
**Pencil on the page:** `wrote` spelling, letter by letter with `people.write_on`.

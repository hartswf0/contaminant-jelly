## The porthole's rule: Mystimation Silhouette (SCION 02)

Seen on screen as three laws, tested by the critic frame by frame:

1. **Whoever is on the far side of the glass is a silhouette.** From Turkey's seat everything outside (bars, fish,
   tunnel, whoever "someone" is) is a flat black shape against a lit teal ground, read by its outline alone, with
   no eyes, no inner drawing, no shading to rescue it. The fish's spots are the one exception, and they are
   **holes punched through the black paper**, with the light coming through, as a shadow-theatre cutter would do
   them. From the river side it turns over: the tin and the trout are engraved paper with their detail, and
   Turkey, inside against his lamp, is the black shape in the round window.
2. **Things move only at pins, and planes only slide.** Every figure is cut pieces hinged at visible pin joints
   with a limit on each joint (Turkey: neck, beak, two wings, wattle; fish: jaw, body, tail, two fins). No
   squash, no morph, no fade-in of a figure: a piece is slid in from the edge of the glass on its own plane, or it
   is laid down by the hand. Scene changes are the **velvet iris of the clue**: a ring of dark red velvet-paper
   leaves closes to a point over the glass and opens on the next scene. One iris move per change, about 0.4 s.
3. **Ruled line over everything.** The lit ground, the water and the stone are engraved: fine parallel ruled
   lines, horizontal for water, following the curve for stone. The bars' upright shadows are the same ruling
   turned vertical, and they fall across whatever is behind them. Colour is held to black, the teal light, brass,
   the velvet red and paper white. **One thing in the whole film is allowed its own colour: the hot Cheeto.**

The rule bends by kind: Probable keeps it plainly; Plausible lets one coloured thing into the black world;
Preposterous opens the black world plane behind plane until it is a whole house.

## Lineage and method

Karel Zeman, *Invention for Destruction* (1958): a film that looks like a steel engraving in motion, made by
putting flat cut-out scenery, ruled with engraver's lines, on planes in front of and behind the moving figures.
We use the **method** on our own subject and copy none of its pictures, characters or words: cut flats ruled
with parallel lines, set at several depths, with a hinged figure working between two of them. Forage, if the
world unit wants printed matter, from nineteenth-century engravings on Wikimedia Commons that are public domain
in their own right (a brook trout plate, a wild turkey plate, masonry or culvert plates, a magic lantern or early
projector plate, a wreck on the sea bed); otherwise every flat is handmade cut paper with ruled lines.

**The made thing is the grating**: nine upright bars cut from black card, each a little out of true, laid one by
one across a stone ring built of separate torn-paper voussoirs, with the punched-spot fish on a wire behind it.

## Part 1 · The pilot's page · 0.0 to 20.0 s (`scene_pilot.py`)

by the world unit, not the photograph): two thin concentric circles and the six small die faces on the ring at
twelve (1), two (2), four (3), six (4), eight (5) and ten o'clock (6). A real pencil with its shadow does the
drawing. The line is grey graphite, wobbly, pressed harder in places. No caption until P3.

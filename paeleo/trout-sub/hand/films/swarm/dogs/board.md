## A · Thesis, defining passage, the forgettable version

### Thesis (three sentences)
A dog that cannot be ordered chooses, again and again, to go under the water for someone who cannot,
and every time it comes back up there is a real thing in its mouth; and when the person says no it
does not argue, it goes down again a different way, and this time somebody who built nothing is
watching what comes up. All the while the cows go to the same water the way they always have, one at
a time down a path nobody chose, so that we can see with our eyes what is new about the dogs: they go
under, and they go together. A child should leave able to say: "you ask, it decides to go, it digs and
sniffs and carries, it calls other dogs who all work at once, not in a line like the cows, and when
you say no it goes back and does it better."

### Formal choices that give it its identity
1. **One line, two worlds.** The surface is a bright line in every shot. Amber and violet above it,
   teal below it. Every crossing is a shot of its own and costs the dog something.
2. **Two ways of working, always in the same frame.** The herd is a LINE (a queue down a hillside,
   one gate, one cow at the water at a time, one slow tempo that never changes). The pack is a FAN
   (from every edge of the frame, into the water in the same four frames, each at its own job at once).
   The line never speeds up; the dog's rounds do. That difference is drawn, never said.
3. **Cows cannot go under; dogs can.** A cow is only ever seen from below as what breaks the line:
   a muzzle, a hoof, a wobbling dark shape. The cows are never in the way out of meanness and are
   never made fun of: twice they do what no dog can (break the ice; warm the eleven).
4. **Everything that goes down comes up with something.** No dog surfaces empty-mouthed, except the
   eleven in S5. That one exception is the plot.
5. **Thinking is a hold.** The dog looks, holds still, then moves. Holds are listed with their seconds.
6. **Every dog is made a different way**, so a pack reads as many makers, never as copies. The cows
   are all made the SAME way (one dark card cut, different patches): a herd is one thing; a pack is many.
7. **The drawing itself stops breathing under the ice.** For those seconds the line boil is off.

### The defining passage (S6, 161.0 to 173.0, twelve seconds; unchanged from the first board)
A wooden retriever comes up with a reel and is proud of it. The old dog, lying across the sill, looks
at it for a second and a half, gets up slowly, turns round, and lies down again with its back to the
reel. Nobody speaks. The builder looks at the old dog's back, looks at the person (no help there),
looks at the lead cow standing behind (she only chews), lowers its ears, and goes down again. It comes
back with the reel changed. The old dog looks over its shoulder, then shifts its hindquarters aside to
make room. The reel is laid down.

Everything before it exists to earn it:
- S1 teaches that a back and a hold mean something (the dog backs off from the hand), and that the
  cows have one gate that everything passes one at a time.
- S2 teaches what "right" looks like: the fish child's own wobbly drawing in a bubble.
- S5 removes the lookers, so a wrong reel gets through; S6 opens on the cost of that.
- The old dog has been on the sill since 95.0 doing nothing, so its one move is the biggest in the film.
- The cows' gate (S1–S4) is a gate you pass by waiting your turn. The old dog is a gate you pass by
  being right. The lead cow, who knows gates, stands and watches this one, and when it is over she
  folds down beside the old dog. That is the weave's last stitch.

Build this passage FIRST (law 3), with the lead dog's ears-down beat (149.0–153.0) attached.

### What would make it competent but forgettable
- Dogs that glide. If digging does not throw gravel, if a shake does not throw water, if a wet page
  does not sag, it is clip-art on a path. The same for cows: a cow has weight; her head nods with each
  foreleg, her bell swings late, she lies down front knees first and gets up hind end first.
- Cows as scenery: a herd pasted behind the action that never touches a dog. Or cows as obstacles or
  as a joke about being slow. Each sequence must have its cow-and-dog moment (part C, marked **WEAVE**)
  played as two animals who notice each other.
- A herd that bunches or a pack that queues. If the cows are ever two abreast on the path, or the pack
  ever arrives nose to tail, the film's one comparison is gone.
- The old dog's turn played fast, or as a head shake, or explained by a symbol.
- The "no" played as scolding and the dog played as sad-then-happy.
- Even intensity. The film needs three places where almost nothing moves (19.5, 118 to 122, 164.0 to 165.5).
- Anything that looks like an interface: boxes, rows, arrows, ticks, counters, a label on an animal,
  lettering on a building. Rejected already, three times.
- Copies: nine dogs that are the lead dog in different colours.
- Music laid under everything to tell us what to feel.

## B · The bar: five tests a stranger applies with the sound off

Each is a yes or no. A critic who built nothing shows the film muted to someone who has not read this
board (or acts as that person with fresh context) and records the answers. JUDGED unless marked.

1. **The retelling test.** After one viewing the stranger says, unprompted: the person asked; the dog
   decided to go; it dug, sniffed and carried; it called other dogs; the person said no; the dog went
   back; an old dog turned away from one and it was done again; and there were cows who could not go
   under. Eight facts. Seven or more is a pass.
2. **The two-ways test.** Ask one question: "How do the cows get to the water, and how do the dogs?"
   A pass is any answer of the kind "the cows go one by one in a line, the dogs all jump in together
   and each does something different". Then ask "Were the cows bad?" A pass is "no", with a reason
   from the picture (she broke the ice / they kept the wet dogs warm / she touched noses).
3. **The silhouette test.** Pause on any ten frames chosen by the critic (at least four pack shots,
   at least three with cows). Fill every character with flat black. The stranger can still point to
   the lead dog (torn ear, collar notch), the old dog (largest, lowest), and the lead cow (one horn
   up, one horn bent down, a bell hanging) in every frame where each is present.
4. **The why-no test.** Pause at 148.0. Ask "is the person going to like it, and why or why not?"
   A pass is "no, it's too neat / it isn't how the fish kid drew it". Pause at 165.5 and ask "what is
   the old dog saying?" A pass is "no" or "not good enough", with no symbol on screen to say it.
5. **The counting test (MEASURED, counted from the picture by the critic).** (a) For every time a dog
   crosses down through the surface on screen there is a later frame of that dog (or its group)
   crossing up with a visible thing in its mouth; the only exceptions are the eleven in S5. (b) No cow
   is ever below the surface line beyond a muzzle or one leg. (c) Stepping through at one frame per
   second (180 frames): lettered words are the title and the end credit only, 14 words; interface
   rectangles, cards, charts, digits, arrows, ticks, labels: zero; lettering on signs, the radio, the
   board, the reels, the bell: none.

Comparison material that can be opened (not inspected by the director; UNKNOWN as a bar until a critic
looks): `assets/films/cowpath-study-refuse.mp4` for a refusal carried by a body,
`assets/films/cowpath-study-shadow.mp4` for light on a made figure, `films/swarm/cowpath/cast.py` for
how a cow was drawn there. Compare acting with acting only. No external reference was available to
inspect; the five tests are the bar, and that limitation is recorded here.

### How a cow moves (acting rules for whoever animates one)
- Walk: slow four-beat, about 45 px a second on the strip; the head nods down as each foreleg lands;
  the bell swings one beat late. Cows never trot. The calf can, and does, and stops badly.
- A cow thinks with her ears (both turn to what she looks at) and stops chewing when she notices.
  Chewing again means she has decided it is all right.
- Lying down ("folding down"): front knees first, a wait, then the hind end sinks and rolls. Getting
  up: hind end first. Both take two seconds.
- Never: a lowered-horn threat, a kick, a stampede, a comic pratfall. The one near-fall (S2, the hoof
  that finds no bottom) is played as real alarm, then dignity.
- The cows' ears turn to the radio. The lead dog's never do.

## F · Sound plan, beat by beat

Principles. Two rooms: ABOVE (dry, close, small sounds, crickets, lamp buzz) and BELOW (everything
through a low-pass at about 700 Hz, slow, a low water drone). The crossing is the swap between them
and is the film's signature sound. No continuous music. The radio is the only music: thin, plucked,
from one place (pan left of centre). Two tempos are audible all film long: the cows are always slow
and low (hoof thuds at walking pace, one dull bell, long breaths); the dogs get quicker. The bell is
`mix.bell` at about 330 Hz, 0.5 s, low gain: a knock, never a chime; pan follows the lead cow.
Silence is used three times: 19.5, 118 to 122, 164.0 to 165.5. All synthesized with `kit.Mix`; names
below are Mix methods. In F-SPLIT both rooms at half level. Nobody here can hear any of this: it is

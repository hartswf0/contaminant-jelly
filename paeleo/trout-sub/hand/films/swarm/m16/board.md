# m16 · board · the MAKING film · porthole 16 · Aft · "Identical twin Turkey Sub waving back"

Trace unit. Total **90.0 s** (960x540, 24 fps). Badge "16" top right on every frame.
Read from: `class_photos/p16_front.jpg`, `class_photos/p16_back.jpg` (looked at only: never on screen, never
committed), the entry with `"porthole": 16` in `../class/transcripts.json`, `../seeds.json` row 15 (clue
"Identical twin Turkey Sub waving back", rule Temporal Double), the deck picture `../stills/p16.jpg`, and the class
film's `films/swarm/c16/WORLD.md` and `board.md`.

One sheet, three children. The **pilot** (name hidden by a fold: no initial is ever lettered) drew small and wrote
three one-line answers. **B.** (first friend) drew the button, wrote two lines and rubbed them out, left the middle
ending empty and wrote one. **W.** (next friend) circled a choice, drew nothing, and wrote one ending in the
middle. Four of six endings are empty. Nobody rolled. The film is those three desks.

## Two warnings about the photographs before the traces

- The lower left of both photographs lies in the photographer's shadow. **Pressure along a line cannot be read
  from them**, so the film never stages "pressing hard, then fading" along a sentence. The pressure differences
  used below hold inside one patch of light: the firm sub against the faint head (side by side in the ring), the
  twice-gone-over "ed", the rubbed-out ghost against the clean lines under it.
- The grey ring and die faces that show faintly on the back are the FRONT's printing showing through the paper,
  and the folded white corner at the top left of the front is another sheet lying on this one. Neither is a trace.

## THE TRACES

Each says where it is on the page and what it **suggests**. Only the mark is certain. "Shown at" names the beat.

### Front · the pilot

1. **The drawing is small and high.** Everything sits in the top third of the ring, between eleven and two
   o'clock; the lower two thirds of the ring are empty paper. *Suggests* a careful small copy begun at the top and
   never grown to fill the space. Shown at P2 and P6 (the pencil crosses the empty part and leaves it).
2. **The sub is drawn firmly, piece by piece.** A leaning box, a dome on top, two small blocks on its left side,
   an upright block on its right, a rounded lug at lower right: every corner overshot, every piece its own closed
   shape. The deck picture's tin has a pipe on top and fittings on both sides. *Suggests* it was copied from the
   small picture one fitting at a time, with looks back and forth. Shown at P2.
3. **The window is the darkest mark on the whole sheet, and nobody is in it.** A small circle in the box,
   scribbled solid, round and round. In the deck picture that window is lit and holds a turkey. *Suggests* the
   pilot drew the twin's window as a dark hole: a sub seen from outside, whose inside cannot be seen. Shown at P2
   (made), P4 (in the thought the window stays dark) and T3 (it is what B. sees through the glass).
4. **A faint turkey head, to the right, looking at the sub.** At about two o'clock, just inside the rim: a beak
   pointing LEFT towards the box, a dot eye, a wattle line, two loose lines trailing down the neck. Much lighter
   than the sub beside it. The deck picture has a ghostly turkey reflected in the glass, pale, at the lower LEFT.
   *Suggests* the reflection was understood as a faint thing and drawn faintly, and that it changed sides on the
   way from the picture to the page. Shown at P4 (it forms on the left, comes apart, forms on the right) and P6.
5. **More faint lines round the sub that are not finished into anything.** A beak-shaped wedge off the sub's
   upper left; a tall thin spike between the sub and the head; a long loose curve hanging under the box; a few
   lines joining them. The same light hand as the head. *Suggests* things begun and left (weed, pipe, a fish, a
   second head: it cannot be told which). Counting the head, there are about three faint shapes round the one firm
   sub, and line 1 then says "three headed". *Suggests, no more than that,* that the faint shapes fed the answer.
   Shown at P4, P6 and P7 (in the thought the three faint shapes are what the necks grow from).
6. **No trout.** The deck picture's largest creature is a brook trout under the tin. It is not on the page, unless
   it is one of the faint unfinished lines. Shown at P6 only by its absence: nothing is added to stand for it.
7. **Everything is inside the ring; the margins are clean.** *(Not staged as an event; the drawing is kept so.)*
8. **Line 1 gives one answer to a stem that asks two questions.** Stem: "Who is outside the glass? What do they
   think our sub is?" Written: `a giant threeheaded monster`. *Suggests* one of the two questions was answered and
   the sheet does not say which: it could be who is out there, or what those outside take the sub for. The film
   does not decide. Shown at P7 (the finger stops at each question mark; one line gets written).
9. **"threeheaded" is one word, and its "ed" is gone over twice.** The last two letters of "headed" are the
   darkest on the line, re-traced. *Suggests* "head" came first and the ending was added or mended. Shown at P7.
10. **Line 2 carries on from the stem.** Stem: "What one thing does Turkey do?" Written: `wave athis twin
    turkey`. No subject, no capital. *Suggests* the printed question was read as the start of the sentence.
    Shown at P8.
11. **"at his" is joined as "athis"; "turkey" jumps.** The last word starts higher than the rest, is larger, and
    its y drops through the printed rule. *Suggests* the hand stopped and lifted before "turkey" and came down in
    a new place. Shown at P8.
12. **"twin turkey" are the clue's words; the waving is turned round.** The printed clue says the twin sub is
    "waving back". The pilot wrote what Turkey does: wave at his twin. *Suggests* a look up at the clue line, and
    a working backwards from "waving back" to who must have waved first. That is already the game: taking
    Turkey's side. Shown at P8 (the eyes go up to the clue and come down).
13. **Line 3 is one unbroken string.** `useinguphisenergy`: no spaces at all, the e of "use" kept in "useing",
    the last letters "rgy" climbing and gone over. The stem prints "use up". *Suggests* the stem's own two words
    were taken down and "ing" joined on, the pencil hardly leaving the paper. Shown at P9.
14. **"his energy" where the stem asks about "our air".** *Suggests* the pilot answered what the waving costs
    Turkey, which is next door to what was asked. Shown at P9 (lettered as written, nothing added).
15. **Every second ruled line is empty; no line starts with a capital; none has a full stop.** Three short
    answers, one line each. Shown at P9 (the last look down the page).
16. **The name cannot be seen.** Shown at P1 to P9 by the left sleeve lying on it.

# THICK PROMPT · The Dogs of Trouttown

A thick prompt carries its own history. It says what to make, and it also says what was already made,
what the person said about it, and what changed because of that. Read the history (part 7) as part of the
instruction. Run this under `SCION_AUTONOMOUS_ANIMATION_STUDIO.md`.

## 1 · Who is asking, and for whom
A person stands at a door and asks for things. An AI named Claude goes and does them. Fourth graders are
going to watch, and they should come away able to say, in their own words, what an AI like Claude does
when it is asked for something. They have never seen a chat window and do not need to.

## 2 · The story in one breath
There is a town under the creek where the fish live like people. There is a porch above it, with a door,
and a person who cannot go under. Only the dog can go between. The person asks; the dog chooses to go;
down in Trouttown the dog digs, sniffs, carries and, when the job is too big, calls a pack. Each dog comes
back up through the water with something in its mouth and lays it across the sill. The person looks,
and sometimes says no. Then the dog goes down again a different way.

## 3 · The world and the cast

| Who | What they are | What they want | How we know them |
|---|---|---|---|
| The person | Whoever asks. Stays on the porch. | To make something for the children. | A lamp-lit doorway, a hand, a radio on the step. Never a face in close-up. |
| The dog | Claude. A paper dog. | To bring back the right thing. | It cannot be ordered. It looks, decides, then moves. Ears and tail tell us first. |
| The pack | Claude's agents. | Each wants one job done. | No two dogs are made the same way: a black cut-out, a wire dog, a wooden toy, a crepe-paper dog, a printed dog. |
| The peoplefish | The trout of Trouttown: a class, a teacher, a diner, a main street. | To be shown as they really said it. | They talk in bubbles with pictures in them, never words. |
| The old dog | The critic. | Nothing. It built nothing. | It lies across the sill, half in, half out. It can turn its back. |
| The water | The line between asking and doing. | | Every crossing is a shot: down through the surface, up through it, shaking off. |

## 4 · The dictionary: what Claude does, what the dog does, what comes back
Every tool call in the film is a dog action with a visible result. Nothing is a label.

| Claude does | The dog does | What comes back to the door |
|---|---|---|
| runs a command | digs in the creek-bed gravel | a part, dripping, in its mouth |
| looks at a file | puts its nose to a page on the Trouttown notice board, ears forward | nothing yet: the dog knows something, and we see it decide |
| writes a file | sets a thing down and presses a paw on it | a paw print stays on it |
| searches | swims out past the last streetlight with a lantern | something from outside town |
| sends a file | carries a film reel up through the surface, shakes, lays it across the sill | the reel, and one frame of the real film it holds |
| starts a swarm | howls once at the waterline | the pack |
| stops a task | one short bark | the pack turns and comes home |

**What each dog in a pack does**

| Agent | Dog | Its one job in Trouttown |
|---|---|---|
| story or trace unit | a herding dog | gathers the fish children's pages without bending one |
| world or set builder | a digger | builds the place the others will stand in |
| scene or act builders | retrievers, three of them | each carries one piece, at the same time |
| editor | a sheepdog | walks the pieces into one line |
| critic | the old dog on the sill | looks at the finished thing and passes it, or turns its back |
| rights reviewer | a bloodhound | sniffs each piece back to where it came from |
| repair | a terrier | goes down the one hole that is wrong |
| sound designer | a dog with its ear to the ground | lays sounds it cannot hear |

A child's sentence is carried in the mouth and never chewed. A blank page is carried just as carefully.

## 5 · The film, in three acts (90 to 130 seconds)

**Act 1 · One dog.** Dusk on the porch. The person speaks; the dog does not come. The person sits, sets the
radio down, and waits; the dog looks at the water and goes. Under the surface, Trouttown at school time.
The dog digs, noses a page on the board, and carries one thing up. The person asks again, sooner. The dog
comes up with it half made. Again, sooner. The pile on the sill is small and wet.

**Act 2 · The pack.** The person says something quiet; we see what it means when the dog turns to the
water and howls. Different dogs arrive from different directions and go under together. Down below,
each does its one job at the same time, around the fish class. Then the water freezes over for a breath
and every dog waits under the ice (the computer restarted). Later, eleven dogs run out of breath and
surface early; they are the ones who were supposed to look. One dog finishes alone.

**Act 3 · No, and again.** The dog lays a finished reel on the sill, pleased. The person holds it to the
lamp and shakes their head. The dog's ears go down. It does not argue. It turns, and this time six
dogs go under at once, and the old dog on the sill watches every reel that comes up, and turns its back
on one. That builder goes down again. The last shot: reels stacked on the sill, the dog lying across it
half in and half out, and under the surface the fish children looking up at the lit door.

## 6 · Hard rules
- **It is a cartoon.** Characters notice, want, try, meet resistance and change. Hold when they think.
- **Cinematic at all times.** Every shot has a place, a light and a reason for where the camera is.
  No chat windows, no cards, no charts, no counters, no tags, no diagrams, no title over black.
- **Almost no words.** At most 20 on screen, title and credit included. At most three spoken lines.
- **Each tool comes back.** Whenever a dog goes under, we see what it returns with.
- **Every dog is different**, and each is built from a different animation SCION (`scion/animation-NN.md`),
  as in IT'S ALL RIGHT, where 54 SCIONs each told one porch scene.
- **Handmake or forage. No style costumes.** 960x540, 24 fps, under 12 MB.

## 7 · The history this prompt carries
Each line is something that was made in this conversation, what the person said, and the rule it left.

| What was made | What the person said | The rule it left |
|---|---|---|
| A film of headings, tables and quotations with a narrator | "Why do you make yhis a PowerPoint instead of cartoon? Why so many words." | Pictures carry it. 20 words. |
| Scenes tagged "animated with SCION 02" that no agent had run | "You aren't using any scions" | A SCION is run whole by an agent, or it is not named. |
| A swarm whose first three steps ran in single file | "Its only one agent....." | Several dogs go under together from the first shot of act 2. |
| A scrolling chat log with counters | "See how abstract this is for a kid?" | No interface on screen. A dog, a person, a door. |
| An edit of borrowed dog footage, where dogs only walk and lie down | "we must build the story" | The dogs dig, sniff, carry and howl in Trouttown. New animation. |

## 8 · What must be true, from the record
These are the real numbers of the conversation this film is about. Show them by quantity in the picture
(holes in the gravel, reels on the sill, dogs in the pack), never as digits.
- The person asked 67 times; 16 of those were the same four words.
- The dog used a tool 605 times: 374 digs, 131 looks, 53 reels carried up, 12 things set down.
- Packs of 10, 51, 37 and 17 dogs; 109 dogs in all.
- The computer restarted once. Eleven dogs stopped once, and they were critics and repairers.
- The dog has never heard one of its own films.

## 8b · What the dog carried up
The reels on the sill are real. `scion/WHAT_WE_MADE.md` lists them in order, with what the person and the
dog saw together: the children's drawings and sentences, the porch dogs, the cows. When a reel is laid on
the sill, the frame that shows on it is a frame of that real film, in that order. The fish children in
Trouttown are the class whose pages these were; what they hold up in their bubbles are their own drawings
(the jaw, the fish and the bell, the bars), redrawn, never the photographs.

## 9 · How to run it
Follow the SCION: thesis and bar; the hardest passage first (the old dog turning its back on a reel, and
the builder going down again); independent critics who built nothing; root-cause repair; every finding
labelled MEASURED, STRUCTURAL, JUDGED or UNKNOWN. Start several agents at once. Do not let a builder
award its own pass. Do not claim to have heard anything.

The Class Portholes

Five Porthole Logs filled in by a fourth-grade class, made into five short films. Every sentence on screen and every sentence spoken is one a child wrote. The spelling is tidied for the caption and kept as written in the tables below. Lines the children left blank stay blank, and no die is thrown.

Children are named by first initial only. The photographs of their sheets are not published.

How to Play the Porthole Game

All five films in one, 6:22. It teaches the four steps first, names the step and the question under every picture, and gives one thing to watch for before each porthole.

How these were made

Each film was made by nine units working in five stages: a story unit and a world unit; three scene units (the pilot, Turkey's seat, the river side); an editor; a critic and a rights reviewer; a repair unit. Forty-five agents across the five films. The pictures are cut paper and pencil drawn in code, with two public-domain plates (Audubon's wild turkey, Denton's brook trout) where a film forages. The voices are three stock Kokoro voices run locally; the songs are passages from DEEP SEA VOYAGE.

Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Three beats, one continuous film.

Beat 1 (0-2.5s), Turkey's seat: the fixed eye-level view inside the riveted steel bread-roll-tin submarine exactly as in the cockpit reference: centered brass six-bolt porthole with a chip at two o'clock, empty ballast slot at lower left, chalk route chart under glass at lower center, brass lamp toggle at lower right, amber lamp overhead, dent in the right wall. Outside the glass: cold blue-green river and one dim rounded metal shape. Only the tip of a feathered turkey wing reaches in from the lower right edge and flips the lamp toggle.

Beat 2 (2.5-5.5s): slow push in to the glass. Outside is the scene of the porthole reference: a second dented steel tin, the same riveted metal as this hull and the size of a roasting pan, tipped on its side on the riverbed, open mouth toward us, stamped 375°F. Its inside glows warm amber. The warmth is upside down: amber water stays pooled low inside the tin under a flat lid of cold blue water; bubbles rise, hit the cold layer and spread sideways in flat bands as in the last reference. Four silver minnows drift in the warm pool. A faint reflection of the turkey's face on the glass gulps.

Beat 3 (5.5-8s), hard cut across the glass to the outside view of the reference: the long riveted metal tin on the riverbed, the turkey small behind the amber porthole, and in the foreground a speckled brook trout beside the small tipped tin. The minnows bask in its glow, then the tin bobs up off the gravel and two minnows pop straight up out of it; the trout flinches back.

The only lettering is 375°F on the small tin; no other text, no labels, no captions.

Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Three beats, one continuous film.

Beat 1 (0-2.5s), Turkey's seat: the fixed eye-level view inside the riveted steel bread-roll-tin submarine exactly as in the cockpit reference: centered brass six-bolt porthole with a chip at two o'clock, empty ballast slot lower left, chalk route chart under glass lower center, brass lamp toggle lower right, amber lamp overhead, dent in the right wall. Outside the glass, in dark blue-green murk, one small pale rectangle of light blinks on, off, on. Only the tip of one turkey wing enters at the lower right corner and flips the brass toggle. No turkey body.

Beat 2 (2.5-5.5s): an amber beam switches on and the camera pushes slowly in to the glass, to the scene of the porthole reference: an old wooden television set with two rabbit-ear antennas, standing tilted on the riverbed. Everything that moves is inside its screen: a yellow sponge with a small face waves a blue cloth over its head in wide arcs, wiping green algae off the inside of the screen glass; the picture flickers and rolls once, then steadies. Two speckled brook trout lie in front of the set, watching, lit by the screen. A faint turkey reflection low left on the porthole glass leans closer. The screen is one flat bold picture, glowing as in the last reference.

Beat 3 (5.5-8s), hard cut across the glass to the outside view of the reference: the riveted metal tin with its handle parked on the pebble riverbed, the turkey small inside the round amber porthole. Beside the tin at the left stands the same television, its screen with the waving sponge glowing toward us: two lit frames side by side, each with one small figure inside. The tin's amber beam falls on the television's back like a headlamp and its picture stops flickering. The two trout turn their heads from the screen to the porthole and back.

Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Three beats, one continuous film.

Beat 1 (0-2.5s), Turkey's seat: the fixed eye-level view inside the riveted steel bread-roll-tin submarine exactly as in the cockpit reference: centered brass six-bolt porthole with hinge, latch and a chip at two o'clock, empty ballast slot at lower left, chalk route chart under glass at lower center, brass lamp toggle at lower right, amber lamp overhead, dent in the right wall. Outside the glass a dark green mesh hangs across murky blue-green water. Only the tip of one feathered turkey wing reaches in from the lower left edge and drops a small round iron ballast weight into the ballast slot. No turkey body, no tail fan.

Beat 2 (2.5-5.5s): the camera pushes slowly in to the glass. The scene of the porthole reference: a chewed green twine net draped across the whole port, frayed ends waving, one ragged hole in the middle. A speckled trout noses through the hole. One taut strand above it snaps in two; that half of the net goes slack and swings toward the glass like a closing loop while the trout slips free. A faint reflection of the turkey's face low on the glass flinches.

Beat 3 (5.5-8s), hard cut across the glass to the outside view of the reference: the riveted metal tin with its top handle hanging in mid-water just above the riverbed, bobbing gently, one green twine line running from the handle straight up out of frame. The turkey is small behind the amber porthole. The cut net trails from the brass latch, its severed ends drifting loose. In the foreground the freed trout looks from the cut twine to the bobbing tin.

Hanging net strands as a soft boundary in blue water as in the last reference; take only that. Bubbles rise throughout. No text, no labels, no captions.

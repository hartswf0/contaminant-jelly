Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Three beats, one continuous film.

Beat 1 (0-2.5s), Turkey's seat: the fixed eye-level view inside the riveted metal bread-roll-tin submarine exactly as in the cockpit reference: centered brass six-bolt porthole with a chip at two o'clock, empty ballast slot at lower left, chalk route chart under glass at lower center, brass lamp toggle at lower right, amber lamp overhead, dent in the right wall. Outside the glass is empty blue-green river. Only the tip of a feathered turkey wing enters from the lower left edge and drops a small grey ballast weight into the slot. The tin settles with a thump and the view sinks to riverbed level.

Beat 2 (2.5-5.5s): slow push in to the glass. The scene of the porthole reference appears: a pale pink sea bream wearing black sunglasses strides left to right across the sandy riverbed between weeds on two thin wooden stick legs, like a model on a catwalk. The bream is a flat cut-paper figure with pencil scales, a different material from the soft watercolor river. Each stick foot lands solidly and kicks up a puff of silt, but when a broad ribbon of water ripples over it, layered as in the last reference, the paper body wavers and goes half transparent, then comes back. A faint reflection of the turkey's face sits low on the glass.

Beat 3 (5.5-8s), hard cut across the glass to the outside view of the reference: the dented steel tin resting on river pebbles, the turkey small behind the amber porthole with only head and chest showing, no legs. In the foreground the paper bream on its stick legs stops, tips its sunglasses down and stares at the tin. The tin rocks and rolls a little forward on the pebbles; a string of bubbles rises from its handle like steam.

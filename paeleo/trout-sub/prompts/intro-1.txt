Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. One single continuous 8-second shot, no cuts, camera locked off.

The fixed eye-level view from the pilot's seat inside a small submarine built from a long oval dented riveted steel tin (metal, never bread), exactly as in the cockpit reference: centered round brass six-bolt porthole with hinge on the left, offset latch on the right and a chip in the rim at two o'clock; empty rectangular ballast slot at lower left; chalk route chart under glass at lower center; brass lamp toggle at lower right; caged amber lamp overhead; dent in the right wall; worn red seat edge at the bottom. Outside the glass is cool blue-green river, dim and silty. The room and its size never change. The pilot is never seen: only the tip of one brown feathered turkey wing rises from the bottom edge of the frame, small, never covering the porthole.

0-2.5s: the wing tip reaches to the lower right and flips the brass toggle. At once an amber beam shines out below the porthole and, through the glass, the silt glows and a notch in a far rock becomes visible.
2.5-5.5s: the wing tip crosses to the lower left and drops one small grey iron weight into the ballast slot; the view through the porthole sinks a little, the riverbed rising in the glass.
5.5-8s: the wing tip settles on the center chart and traces the white chalk route line along the glass, then rests there, the amber beam still on.

From the last reference take only the staging: one small hand at the frame edge serving a large round opening; do not take its brown color or anything drawn in it. Bubbles drift past the glass. No text, no labels, no captions.

Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Three beats, one continuous film.

Beat 1 (0-2.5s), Turkey's seat: the fixed eye-level view inside the riveted steel bread-roll-tin submarine exactly as in the cockpit reference: centered brass six-bolt porthole with a chip at two o'clock, empty ballast slot at lower left, chalk route chart under glass at lower center, brass lamp toggle at lower right, amber lamp overhead, dent in the right wall. Outside the glass is blue-green murk and one pale square. Only the tip of a feathered turkey wing reaches in from the lower right corner and flips the brass lamp toggle.

Beat 2 (2.5-5.5s): an amber beam lights the water and the camera pushes in to the glass. The scene of the porthole reference fills the port: an old cream posterboard chart pinned in a weathered wooden frame with rusty corner plates, a golden domed saucer craft painted at its centre with a round port like this one, ringed by small red, blue and green arrows and compass marks. The arrows slide inward and point at the craft's port and hinge; then black dashed lines draw themselves across the saucer, dividing it into sections like cuts. A faint reflection of the turkey's face on the glass tilts.

Beat 3 (5.5-8s), hard cut across the glass to the outside view of the reference: the riveted metal tin on the riverbed with its handle on top, the turkey small behind the amber porthole. In the foreground the same framed chart stands on two posts in the pebbles, its painted saucer glowing gold in the beam. One speckled brook trout lays a pebble at its foot and bows; a second trout taps a fin on a blank paper tag with a drawn empty hourglass that hangs on a string from the frame's corner.

The posterboard is rubbed, brown and handled as in the last reference. Bubbles rise throughout. The chart has only arrows and symbols, no words. No text, no labels, no captions.

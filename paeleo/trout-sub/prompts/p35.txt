Hand-inked watercolor storybook animation in the ink line, paper grain and palette of the reference images. Three beats, one continuous film.

Beat 1 (0-2.5s), Turkey's seat: the fixed eye-level view inside the riveted steel bread-roll-tin submarine exactly as in the cockpit reference: centered brass six-bolt porthole with a chip at two o'clock, empty ballast slot at lower left, chalk route chart under glass at lower center, brass lamp toggle at lower right, amber lamp overhead, dent in the right wall. Outside the glass is blue-green murk and one leaning dark post. Only the tip of a feathered turkey wing reaches in from the lower left corner and drops a small iron ballast weight into the slot, like posting a letter.

Beat 2 (2.5-5.5s): the camera pushes in to the glass, then locks and does not move. The scene of the porthole reference fills the port: a dented blue tin mailbox painted "#44" on a leaning wooden post, its door hanging open, a bulging tan canvas sack inside spilling white envelopes with red wax seals. One envelope tumbles out; a speckled brook trout wearing a tiny grey postal cap catches it in its mouth and carries it straight toward the glass, while the sack sags and slumps like a bag of washing. A faint reflection of the turkey's face on the glass tilts.

Beat 3 (5.5-8s), hard cut across the glass to the outside view of the reference, camera locked: the riveted metal tin on the riverbed with its handle on top, the turkey small behind the amber porthole, the open "#44" mailbox on its post at the left edge. The same trout, envelope in mouth, hovers at the tin; loose envelopes drift along the current and are drawn toward the hull, where the tin's rear flap hangs open like a delivery van door.

Slanting shafts of sun through the water as in the last reference. Bubbles rise throughout. The only lettering is "#44" on the mailbox; no other text, no labels, no captions.

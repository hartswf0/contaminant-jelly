How a Language Model Makes a Film

A 45-minute lesson on SCION prompts. Grades 4 and up. No AI account and no device needed.

What students learn

A language model can make a film in two ways. It can guess pixels from a caption. Or it can follow written rules and write code that draws each frame. A SCION prompt is the set of rules. It has four parts. Students write one, hand it to another group, and get judged by someone who built nothing.

You need

The film How a Language Model Makes a Film (2:22). One copy of the sheet My SCION for each pair. Pencils.

Words to teach

ClaimOne sentence the film must make true. A viewer can check it.
Hard sceneThe one moment that proves the claim. Make it first. If it fails, the film fails.
JobOne task for one maker. Each maker hands over one finished thing.
JudgeA person who built nothing. The judge says pass, nearly, or not yet, and points to the reason.

Steps

5 minWatchPlay the film. Ask: who is holding the chalk? Write every answer on the board. Do not correct any answer yet.
5 minName the partsWrite the four parts on the board. For each part, ask students to say what it was in the film they just watched.
15 minWrite a SCIONPairs fill in the sheet for a film that lasts ten seconds and shows one sentence. They can use a sentence from the porthole game or write a new one. They draw the hard scene in the box.
10 minSwap and judgeEach pair passes its sheet to another pair. The new pair is the judge. The judge reads the claim, looks at the hard scene, and circles pass, nearly, or not yet. The judge writes one thing to fix.
10 minRepair and labelSheets go back. Each pair changes one thing. Then they label their claim: measured, judged, or unknown. Ask again: who is holding the chalk? Compare with the first answers on the board.

What to look for

A claim that a stranger can check by watching. A hard scene that shows the claim without words. A judge who points to a place on the page and does not say only "good job". A repair that changes one thing.

Go further

Act out the hard scene for ten seconds while the judge watches. Or draw it as a flipbook of six pages. Or give the finished SCION to an AI tool and judge what comes back with the same sheet.

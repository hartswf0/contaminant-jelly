# Feedstock · Welcome to Trout Town

## What the frames show
- **Medium.** Photographic-looking rendered imagery, not drawing: a40 and a41 read as staged colour film stills of a built underwater set; a42 is smoother and more illustrated, with rounded toy-like figures.
- **Line.** None. Form comes from light, focus and soft edges; the only hard edges are posts, boards and trunks against bright water.
- **Palette.** Deep teal water (#0F4A55), bottle green (#1F5A3A), blue-black shadow (#0A1A22), pale sun-white (#EAF4E4), amber lamp (#F2B25A), warm wood brown (#6B3A1E), cream cloth and paper (#E9DFC2), safety orange (#E8662A), cyan shaft (#3FB4D8).
- **Light.** One strong source per frame. a40: sun breaking through a rippled surface overhead, falling in a soft column. a41: a single hanging shade making an amber pool on a counter, green murk beyond. a42: fanned sunbeams from the upper right through bare trunks.
- **Staging.** A resident at a job, mid-frame or right of centre, tool in hand (long-handled hoe, fork, a sparking rod). Tall uprights (posts, hanging weed, trunks) bracket the figure. Small props crowd the working surface.
- **Depth.** Deep. Dark near frame edge, lit middle ground, then blue haze swallowing the far uprights.
- **Texture.** Film grain, slight vignette, weathered plank, leaf, cloth, glossy fish skin, drifting specks.

## How it moves
Inference only; the stills show no blur. The surface ripple in a40, the bubble string in a41 and the beams in a42 suggest slow ambient motion: light shimmering, weed swaying, specks and bubbles rising, while the worker makes small unhurried gestures at a task. Probably long held shots rather than cuts.

## LAW
Through the glass, the view looks photographed rather than drawn: a deep, grainy colour-film picture with no ink outline, soft focus falling away into blue-green haze. One light only, either sun dropping in pale shafts from a rippled surface overhead or a single amber lamp pooling on a work surface, with everything else sinking to dark teal. Tall uprights stand either side of the subject and fade with distance. The subject is caught in the middle of ordinary work and moves slowly and slightly, while the light shimmers, weed sways and a thin string of bubbles and specks rises.

## Fits
Suits places where somebody works or lives: counters, gardens, yards, streets, drowned buildings, objects read by where the light falls on them, anything needing real depth and weight. Does not suit flat graphic gags, snap timing, diagrams, lettering, or silhouettes that must stay unreadable, since the photographic light explains too much.
